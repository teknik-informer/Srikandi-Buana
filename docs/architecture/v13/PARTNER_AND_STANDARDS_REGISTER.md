# SRIKANDI BUANA v13 — Partner, Government and Standards Register

**Purpose:** convert external program obligations into versioned, traceable work plans and evidence checklists.  
**Status:** initial source register; not a confirmation that SRIKANDI BUANA has been accepted, enrolled, approved or certified by any listed organisation.

## 0. Current engagement status — ToT, not a signed programme

The group has clarified that current partner engagement is at the **Training of Trainers (ToT) / early engagement stage**. There is no signed programme agreement or contract yet, and the group does not currently hold digital copies of the relevant programme documents. This is a group-supplied operational status, not a status issued by the partners.

Accordingly, the platform should record partner contacts, actual ToT events, attendance, learning, action plans and requested/received documents. It must not create an active programme, infer grant funding, populate participant targets or report certification readiness as achieved. The templates are generic preparation tools; partner-specific obligations are configured only when official guidance and agreement documents are received.

## 1. Partner/program profiles

### CARE Indonesia

CARE Indonesia's published program priorities include gender justice and social inclusion, environmental/climate/biodiversity justice, women's economic empowerment, crisis preparedness and resilient systems. Its published 18 March 2026 article describes a CARE Indonesia–SUCAFINA collaboration, supported by JDE Peet's and RVO NL, establishing Community Productive Business Groups (KUPM) for women coffee farmers in West and Central Java. The article names KUPM Mandalawangi and KUPM Makmur Lestari in Kemiri Village, Temanggung, and describes group statutes/bylaws, governance, group-managed savings/loans and collective business activities.

**Platform capabilities to prepare**
- Group constitution, AD-ART, assembly record, member agreement and governance structure.
- Member/farmer registration with purpose-specific consent and program eligibility.
- Baseline household/economic profile with data minimisation and restricted access.
- Financial literacy and business training curricula, attendance and pre/post assessment.
- Group savings and lending records only where the group has adopted a documented policy and the activity is legally authorised; track member contribution, approval, disbursement, repayment, balance and reconciliation separately. Do not present the system as a bank or licensed financial institution.
- Collective business plans, operating costs, sales, income and member benefit distribution.
- Women's economic empowerment indicators, participant voice, feedback, complaints and safeguarding.
- Program outputs/outcomes with baseline, target, actual, source, verification and reporting period.

**Do not assume:** the group is one of the named KUPM, has received the same grant, has the same eligibility, or is authorised to operate savings/loan activities. The active agreement and written partner instructions are the controlling source.

Official references:
- CARE Indonesia — Program Kami: https://careindonesia.or.id/id/program-kami/
- CARE Indonesia — KUPM and women coffee farmers (18 March 2026): https://careindonesia.or.id/en/establishing-community-productive-business-groups-to-support-the-economic-empowerment-of-women-coffee-farmers/

### SUCAFINA

Sucafina Indonesia publicly describes partnerships with farmers, cooperatives, collectors, mills and exporters in several Indonesian coffee origins. Its IMPACT sourcing program identifies five sustainability areas: living income, regenerative agriculture, carbon emissions, human rights and forest conservation. Its Indonesia page also describes digitised data collection and traceability, carbon/deforestation risk tools, regenerative trial plots, agronomic and post-harvest training, agricultural inputs, seedling distribution, community nurseries and financing support.

**Platform capabilities to prepare**
- Farmer/producer and farm/plot master registry, with controlled access to identity and geolocation.
- Traceable delivery records from farmer to collection point, processor, warehouse, lot and shipment.
- Harvest, quality, processing and mass-balance evidence by lot.
- Training, extension visit, seedling/input distribution and adoption records.
- Living-income input data with methodology, reference period and privacy protection; never infer household living income from sales alone.
- Regenerative practice and plot-level evidence; define the practice and verification method.
- Carbon activity data (fuel, electricity, fertiliser, transport, processing) with calculation boundary and factor source; do not show a carbon footprint result until a method and factor set are approved.
- Human-rights, labour, health-and-safety, grievance and remediation records with restricted case access.
- Forest-risk and land-use evidence with data source, date, coordinate quality and review status.
- Buyer delivery specifications, price/premium/discount, quality result, invoice and payment reconciliation.

**Do not assume:** an IMPACT participation status or buyer contract from a discussion or general offer. Record the exact Sucafina business unit, program name, contract, commodity scope, locations, required data fields and reporting calendar supplied to the group.

Official reference:
- Sucafina — About PT Sucafina Indonesia and IMPACT: https://www.sucafina.com/NA/lp/about-pt-sucafina-indonesia

### Rainforest Alliance

The Rainforest Alliance Certification Program requires the organisation to use the applicable current standard, assurance rules and annexes. Its current phased-implementation page states that the documents effective from 1 March 2026 include Supply Chain Requirements v1.5 and Traceability Annex v1.4, alongside the corresponding farm/regenerative requirements and annex versions. The Group Member Registry (GMR) is a binding template for farm certificate holders to record group member and farm information for compliance and audit preparation. The Rainforest Alliance describes group certification for farms that share a common management system; Group Management is responsible for implementing the group's internal management system and assuring member-farm compliance.

**Platform capabilities to prepare**
- Group management role, documented certification scope, farm/site register and member farm status.
- Internal Management System (IMS) with current controlled standard documents and applicability decisions.
- Farm/member risk assessment, internal inspection schedule, findings, corrective actions, evidence and closure verification.
- Member/farm registry export mapped to the current official Group Member Registry template.
- Plot geolocation and farm boundaries with mapping date, method, accuracy and verification.
- Traceability, transaction records, volume reconciliation and permitted claim/segregation controls.
- Applicable social and environmental risk controls, training, grievance and remediation.
- Certificate, certification-body, audit, scope, validity, suspension and renewal references.

**Control rule:** version every standard, checklist and annex. A checklist copied from an expired version must not be silently reused. The user interface must display the source version, effective date and replacement status. An internal checklist is a self-assessment tool, not an official certification outcome.

Official references:

- Rainforest Alliance — Supply Chain Requirements v1.5: https://knowledge.rainforest-alliance.org/docs/rainforest-alliance-supply-chain-requirements-v15
- Rainforest Alliance — Traceability Annex v1.4: https://knowledge.rainforest-alliance.org/docs/traceability-annex-v14

### Ministry of Agriculture — Ministry and local agriculture services

The official legal database lists Permentan 67/PERMENTAN/SM.050/12/2016 on farmer-institution development as in force and says it repealed Permentan 82/2013. The official e-RDKK system says farmer-group IDs must match the group master data originating from SIMLUHTAN; village codes, subsector and commodity must also match the official master data. A Ministry technical instruction for the 2025 subsidised fertiliser process identifies accurate farmer KTP name, KTP address, date/place of birth, plot coordinates and area as data items to check. These fields must only be collected when required and with appropriate notice/access control.

**Platform capabilities to prepare**
- Farmer-institution profile and member register.
- Village, hamlet, region, farmer-group, subsector and commodity reference data.
- SIMLUHTAN group/member reference, responsible extension officer/BPP and validation date.
- Farm coordinate and area data, with map provenance and verification.
- Annual crop planning, input requirements and extension support.
- e-RDKK preparation/export using the current official template and current commodity/region master values, where the group and farmer qualify.
- Record of who submitted or corrected the data, official receipt/approval, and any rejection/correction.

**Important limits:** the platform does not have an assumed direct Ministry API or authority to register a group in SIMLUHTAN/e-RDKK. The group must confirm eligibility, templates and submission through the relevant official channel and local extension office. Not every commodity or farmer is eligible for every program or subsidised input.

## 2. Cross-cutting legal and international standards

### Indonesian personal data protection

UU 27/2022 on Personal Data Protection is in force. The system must support purpose limitation, data minimisation, notice, access/correction requests, consent withdrawal where applicable, controlled disclosure and retention/deletion rules. National ID, personal financial data, household details, precise farm locations, worker and safeguarding information require stricter controls than ordinary group activity data.

Reference: https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/229798/uu-no-27-tahun-2022

### EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)

The European Commission's current page lists application from 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators/traders and 30 June 2027 for micro and small enterprises. Covered products require due diligence and essential information including plot geolocation. Responsibility depends on the role and size of the operator placing the product on the EU market or exporting it; an Indonesian farmer group must establish the data-sharing obligations in its buyer/export contract.

**Platform preparation:** plot geolocation and quality control, producer/source linkage, harvest/production records, legality evidence, lot traceability, data export, buyer reference and issue/correction log. The system is a data-readiness tool; it must not claim EUDR certification or submit an importer's Due Diligence Statement unless formally authorised.

Reference: https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/forests/deforestation/eu-deforestation-regulation-eudr_en

### GS1 traceability principles

The GS1 Global Traceability Standard structures traceability around Critical Tracking Events (CTEs) and Key Data Elements (KDEs). For the group, relevant events include farm production/harvest, delivery/receiving, weighing, quality assessment, transformation, packing, shipping and transfer of custody. Each event needs a unique identifier, timestamp, location, actor, product/lot, quantity/unit, source/destination and supporting evidence.

References:
- https://support.gs1.org/support/solutions/articles/43000734535-what-is-the-gs1-traceability-standard-
- https://www.gs1.org/sites/default/files/docs/traceability/GS1_Global_Traceability_Standard_i2.pdf

### ISO 22005

ISO 22005:2007 provides principles and basic requirements for designing and implementing traceability in feed and food chains; ISO lists it as current after review in 2022. Use it as a design reference for objectives, system scope, product/component history and traceability procedures. It does not certify the group or replace food-safety and market-specific obligations.

Reference: https://www.iso.org/standard/36297.html

### GLOBALG.A.P. and other buyer-specific standards

GLOBALG.A.P. IFA v6 can be relevant for in-scope crop operations and specific buyers. It is a separate certification pathway with its own applicable scope, approved certification body and annual audit. It must not be marked applicable to every crop or every coffee program by default. Other standards (SNI, organic, Fairtrade, 4C, buyer protocols or ISO-based systems) are added only after verifying the commodity scope, current edition, local recognition and contract requirement.

Reference: https://globalgap.org/what-we-offer/solutions/ifa-fruit-and-vegetables/

## 4. Standard register data fields

Each standard/control record must include:

- Issuer and scheme name.
- Document title, document code, language and version.
- Publication, binding/effective, review and expiry/replacement dates.
- Applicable commodities, farms, sites, supply-chain actors, roles and countries.
- Requirement/control ID and exact source reference/page/section.
- Requirement text or a lawful short summary; link to the official source instead of copying a protected standard in full.
- Applicability decision and written reason.
- Evidence type and retention rule.
- Owner, internal verifier, external verifier and review date.
- Current result, issue/CAPA, closure evidence and approval.
- Change log linking the prior version to the replacement.

A new version must not overwrite the prior assessment. Preserve which version the organisation used when a past audit or donor report was prepared.

## 5. Program-specific configuration rules

The program manager creates a program record, chooses the correct partner profile, then attaches the actual signed agreement, current implementation guidance and reporting template. A partner profile is only a starting template. It cannot populate assumed targets, participant counts, funding or promised outcomes.

Before program activation, the program must pass:
1. Written mandate/offer and named counterpart confirmed.
2. Eligibility and geographic scope reviewed.
3. Budget, fund restrictions and payment schedule approved.
4. Indicator definitions, baseline and target signed off.
5. Personal-data and photo/location-sharing purposes approved.
6. Safeguarding and grievance channel assigned.
7. Delivery team, procurement authority and reporting calendar set.
8. Evidence and verification responsibilities assigned.
